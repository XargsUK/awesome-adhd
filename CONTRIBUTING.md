# Suggesting a resource

I curate this list by hand. I check each suggestion before it goes on, and I turn most of them down. Read this before you open an issue and you'll know which way yours is likely to go.

## Apps and tools

- It has been public for at least six months. Count from the store release date, the first tagged release, or the day the site went live. If it's newer than that I'll close the issue and ask you to come back later. A lot of apps and sites vanish inside their first six months, and I don't want to send readers to something that won't be there next year.
- Someone looks after it. It works today, and the author has shipped an update or fixed a bug in the last six months. A small tool that's finished and works is fine.
- People other than the author use it. Around 25 store ratings, a couple of hundred GitHub stars, or people recommending it somewhere the author doesn't control, such as a subreddit thread or a review. One of those is enough.
- The pricing is plain. Tell me what's free and what costs money, with the prices. The list marks each app as free, freemium or paid, and I check your description against the store listing and the pricing page.
- The privacy policy sits under the product's own name, agrees with the store's privacy label, and says where your data goes. If the app sends text or audio to an AI provider, the policy names the provider.
- It's for ADHD. Either the author made it for people with ADHD, or people with ADHD recommend it and you can say why.
- It adds something to its section. Look at the [README](README.md) first. If the section has three visual timers, a fourth needs to do something they don't.
- The link opens the thing itself, with no waitlist, survey or sign-in wall in front of it.

I don't list apps with a weekly subscription, a free trial that takes a card up front, or payment by crypto only. ADHD readers are more likely than most to forget to cancel.

## Articles, websites and creators

- A named person or organisation stands behind the content. For anything covering health, medication, money or legal rights, I want to see their background too.
- I don't list articles that sit on a product's marketing site.
- Self-assessments and screening questionnaires come from bodies with clinical standing, such as CHADD, NIMH or ADHD UK, or have a published validation study.

## If you made it

You can suggest your own work, and most people who open an issue here are doing that. Say so in the issue. The bar above is the same for you as for anyone else.

## Before you open an issue

Search the open and closed issues for the name. Send one suggestion per resource, through either [GitHub](https://github.com/XargsUK/awesome-adhd/issues/new?assignees=XargsUK&labels=suggestion&projects=&template=%F0%9F%93%9A-new-resource-suggestion.md&title=%5BNEW+RESOURCE%5D) or the form on [awesomeadhd.com](https://www.awesomeadhd.com/suggest), not both.

Include the first release date, the links, the full pricing and whether you're connected to the project. I close issues that leave those out.

## What happens next

I go through suggestions in batches, so a reply can take a month or more. If I close yours I'll say why. If the reason was age or too few users, open a new issue once that has changed and link the old one.

Meeting every point above doesn't guarantee a place. I have to think it's worth a reader's time as well.

## After something is listed

I remove entries when the link dies, the author abandons the project, or the pricing changes to something I don't list. If you spot one, open an issue.
